## Corrections made after reading the primary text

* The DNV report attributes a critical Weber number of 12.5 to the Yellow Book. The Yellow Book itself writes d = C_ds sigma / (u_f^2 rho_a) with **C_ds = 15** recommended. The tool uses 15.
* The DNV report's Kletz and Lautkaski correlations are not the Yellow Book method. The Yellow Book integrates the droplet evaporation while it falls (2.149, 2.150) and gives a rain-out mass directly.
* Misprints in the Yellow Book found while reproducing its worked example: (2.140) prints (1 + Phi_e) where (1 - Phi_e) reproduces the table; (2.146) prints ln(1 - P_s/P_a), negative, while the example's k_B is positive (the module uses minus that logarithm); the example's "d_M = 47 mm" is 0.47 mm (the zeroth-order result 0.29 mm times the convection factor 1.63) and its quoted Re_d of 3671 does not follow from either. These are recorded in the `yb_spray` docstring. The example also uses the number 1.97e-4, labelled dynamic viscosity, as a kinematic viscosity in Re_f; the choice between (2.143a) and (2.143b) is unaffected.
* Heat of combustion: the Annex net value for propane is 46.013 MJ/kg (the earlier 46.33 was my derivation from a NIST higher value and water's latent heat; replaced).
* LPG pool burning rate: Yellow Book Table 6.5 gives 0.099 kg/m2 s for large pools; Mizner (1982) measured 0.13 for a 20 m propane-propene pool; the Burgess form gives about 0.11 for propane. The tool uses Table 6.5 with the diameter correction.
* **The two discharge bases differ by a factor of four for propane at 12 bar.** The Yellow Book (2.5.3.4) treats a hole in the liquid space as liquid outflow, Bernoulli with the full pressure difference, and flashes the liquid after the exit; the omega method treats the same hole as flashing, choked flow. For the 25 mm hole: 9.87 kg/s against 2.34 kg/s. The tool computes both and lets you choose which drives the fire calculation.

### Validation against measurements (EEC STEP propane flashing jets, as reported in HSE CRR 403/2002)

Predicted initial droplet diameter by the tool (Yellow Book 2.143b) against the measured Sauter mean diameter at 60 mm downstream. Pressures taken as absolute; the 5 mm orifice (droplet size in the model does not depend on orifice size once the jet state is fixed by the pressure).

| Storage pressure | Measured SMD | Liquid-orifice basis | Omega basis | CCPS flashing correlation as printed by DNV (not used by the tool) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 bar | 39 to 49 micrometres | 56 | 19 | 221 |
| 11 bar | 30 to 31 | 20 | 8.7 | 166 |
| 17 bar | below 30 | 12 | 5.7 | 138 |

The Yellow Book formula on the liquid-orifice jet state is within a factor of two of the measured means; on the omega state it under-predicts by two to three times; the CCPS flashing correlation as implemented over-predicts about five times, so it is only a comparison in the tool. The measurements are mean sizes after some evaporation, not the initial size, so this is a consistency check, not a calibration; it is encoded in `tests/test_source_term.py` (class `StepValidation`).

## Sixth batch: two documents the owner supplied (2026-11-10)

- **RIVM report 620550005/2012**, Kooi and Uijt de Haag, "A comparison of jet fire models for horizontal two-phase and liquid releases" (grade A for the tabulated values, read from the supplied PDF). It compares the Dutch SAFETI-NL model (JFSH-Cook, the family `jet_flame` follows), the Shell Barker (LPG) and Cracknell (generic) models against five experiments. Two are propane LPG (Project AA tests 3026 and 3029, 97 % propane, 52 mm orifice); the others are crude oil (BFETS-2) and butane (JIVE 8051).
  - Table 5 values: test 3026, 16.1 kg/s, expanded velocity 153 m/s (JFSH): measured flame length 31.1 m, JFSH-Cook 38.3, Barker 37.7, Cracknell 34.3; measured surface emissive power 331 kW/m2, JFSH 253; fraction radiated 0.35 JFSH, 0.27 Barker, 0.30 Cracknell. Test 3029, 18.0 kg/s, 146 m/s: measured 30.7 m, JFSH 49.4, Barker 39.4, Cracknell 34.9; measured SEP 294, JFSH 197.
  - Conclusions (section 3.5): Cracknell is the best generic model for flame size and shape; Barker may be better for volatile liquids such as propane; JFSH-Cook better for non-volatile releases; the fraction radiated is uncertain and JFSH-Cook is the most conservative. JFSH-Cook ignores buoyancy.
- **Check of `jet_flame.chamberlain` against Project AA** (not in the toolkit's tests, finding only): run horizontal (angle 0), wind 3.7 and 2.0 m/s, jet velocity from the report, jet density from the toolkit flash, the model gives flame lengths of 55 m (test 3026) and 69 m (test 3029) against measured 31.1 and 30.7 m and JFSH-Cook 38.3 and 49.4. It therefore overpredicts length by about 1.8 to 2.2 times for horizontal two-phase LPG jets. The toolkit's angle factor (1 - 0.00607 (theta - 90)) raises a horizontal flame by 55 %; with the vertical factor the length is 35.6 m. The two-phase Cook modification and buoyancy are not implemented (the model is the pure-vapour Chamberlain form). Surface emissive power comes out at 96 (Chamberlain fraction 0.24) or 139 kW/m2 (Cook fraction 0.35) against measured 331. The worked example in this ledger is a vertical jet, where this bias is not demonstrated, but jet lengths for horizontal or two-phase releases should not be taken from this model without a check.
- **PHMSA-2011-0075-0003, DNV Phast UDM 6.6 validation submission** (DNV to PHMSA, 25 October 2010; grade B, qualitative). ### Validation against Project AA propane jets (RIVM 620550005/2012 Table 5; toolkit chain run 2026-11-10)

Conditions: 97 % propane, 52 mm orifice, vessel 7.53 bar(a) at 5.5 C (test 3026) and 7.34 bar(a) at 3.2 C (test 3029), 67 m line, horizontal release, wind 3.7 and 2.0 m/s. The liquid is subcooled by about 2 bar, so the omega (saturated) route does not apply; the Yellow Book liquid route is used. Jet state is computed at the measured release rate.

| Quantity | Test 3026 measured | Barker / Cracknell / JFSH-Cook | Toolkit | Test 3029 measured | Toolkit |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Release rate, kg/s | 16.1 | - | 34 to 55 (orifice, Cd 0.62 to 1) | 18.0 | 34 to 55 |
| Expanded jet velocity, m/s | not reported | 79 / 76 / 153 | 75 | not reported | 74 |
| Flame length, m | 31.1 | 37.7 / 34.3 / 38.3 | 59.6 horizontal factor, 38.5 vertical factor | 30.7 | 73.6, 47.6 |
| Surface emissive power, kW/m2 | 331 | 231 / 178 / 253 | 112 horizontal, 265 vertical | 294 | 97, 232 |
| Droplet diameter, micron | not reported | - | 34 | not reported | 34 |

Reading: (1) the expansion step agrees with the Barker and Cracknell velocities (75 against 69 to 79), not with the DNV JFSH value of 153 m/s. (2) The release rate is over-predicted 2 to 3.5 times because the toolkit has no 67 m line: the real flow is limited by line friction and flashing in the pipe (the orifice would see a pressure below the vapour pressure), and a saturated-liquid-in-a-vessel model cannot reproduce it. Feed the measured rate to the jet steps, as done above. (3) Flame length is over-predicted 1.9 to 2.4 times with the horizontal-release factor; the factor is 1.55 and is the whole discrepancy for test 3026 (38.5 m with the vertical factor, 24 % high). The sign of the horizontal angle correction in DNV eq (4) should be checked against the JFSH document. (4) Emissive power follows the length error: low by 3 times (horizontal factor), low by 20 % (vertical factor). (5) Droplet size of 34 micron is of the same order as the STEP propane measurements (30 to 49 micron) at different conditions.
Not validated: the omega discharge (no experiment here with a short hole and saturated liquid), rain-out and pool fire (no data reported), probit and frequency (not measurable). The vertical-release flame in the leak study therefore has an unquantified bias; the horizontal tests show the model can be 24 % to 2.4 times long depending on the angle factor.

Its Figure 5 shows that the DNV/Cook model gives up to 30 % higher flux than the 1D model for horizontal fires and within 15 % for vertical ones, because the 1D model includes buoyancy. The exit pressure, orifice, velocity and wind of the INERIS tests are not in the paper (reference [10], Bertrand and Durussel 2005, not obtained).
- **Check of `jet_flame.chamberlain` against those fires** (vertical, theta = 90, wind 3 m/s and exit velocity 20 to 40 m/s assumed because unpublished): propane 1.5 kg/s 16.2 m (+1 %), propane 4.1 kg/s 24.6 m (+3 %), butane 1.5 kg/s 16.1 m (+1 %), butane 3 kg/s 21.5 m (-7 %); Cook 3.2 GW natural gas at an assumed 450 m/s: 61.7 m (+3 %). All within 10 %. Sensitivity: without wind the propane 1.5 kg/s length is 25.1 m (+57 %), and at 150 m/s the length is -12 % to -20 %, so the agreement depends on the assumed wind and velocity. If the angle factor were applied to a vertical jet (the alternative convention), these would be +50 to +60 % high; the vertical convention is therefore supported. The horizontal amplification (factor 1.546 at theta = 0) remains unsupported (Project AA, JIVE). Natural gas horizontal (Johnson 1083) is +12 % to +36 % at 200 to 600 m/s, not resolved.
- **Osman et al. (GDF Suez), "A review of damages observed after catastrophic events in the mid-stream gas industry compared to consequences modelling tools", Hazards 25, 2015** (grade B for use here). Natural-gas pipeline and storage accidents, damage-derived heat-flux distances (for example 25 kW/m2 at 65 m, 13 kW/m2 at 90 m in one accident; 5 kW/m2 at about 370 m in another) compared with PERSEE, ORDER and PHAST. Conclusion: good agreement, PHAST under-predicts 25 kW/m2 distances for buried pipe ruptures, and the authors say the data are not accurate enough to validate models. Natural gas only, so no direct use for LPG.
- **ph67fm.pdf** is the front matter and contents of Australian Journal of Physics, volume 20 (1967): no combustion or fire paper. Nothing to use. If a specific paper in that volume was intended, send the paper itself.

## Eighth batch: DNV JFSH theory and validation and other documents the owner supplied (2026-11-10)

- **DNV, "Theory: Jet Fire" (JFSH), December 2023** (grade A, read from the owner-supplied PDF). Equations (1) to (4), (15) to (18), (20) to (23), (25), (28), (31) to (33) and (38) match `jet_flame`. Eq (4) defines theta_jv as the angle between the release direction and the wind vector (theta_j, the angle between the hole axis and the horizontal, when crosswind is ignored): 90 for a vertical release (factor 1), 0 for a horizontal release along the wind (factor 1.546). This settles the vertical convention. Differences from the toolkit: the Cook model uses a simpler frustum-base width (eq 26, exp(-7.5 R)) and, for two-phase and liquid releases, replaces the jet density in the width correlations by the saturated vapour density (eq 58; the effective source diameter itself comes out the same); the flammable mass is cut to m = Md min(1, 3(1 - rainout)) (eq 59); the maximum SEP is 350 kW/m2 (as implemented). Not implemented here: Johnson horizontal-vapour model, side/end radiant fractions, Miller model, ground-impingement rotation.
- **DNV, "Validation: Jet Fire", December 2023** (grade A). States that JFSH-Cook is generally conservative for liquid and two-phase jets, with flame length mostly within 30 to 33 % of measurement (average absolute deviation 43.7 % in the original set, tests 3026 and 3029 excepted), SEP within -30 % on average, radiation within 40 %. Revised validation Table 13 (Phast DISC/UDM/JFSH predictions): B3026 flame 38.9 m, B3029 50.0 m, SA8051 33.2 m against measured 31.1, 30.7 and 18.3 m (+25 %, +63 %, +81 %); post-expansion state for B3026 velocity 152.7 m/s, radius 0.059 m, liquid fraction 0.768 (toolkit vapour fraction 0.235 at 153 m/s is the same state). The line-rupture model with the upstream pressure tuned to the measured rate is used for these tests, which confirms that the orifice model is the wrong source term for line-fed releases.
- **Check of the toolkit with DNV's own post-expansion inputs** (Cook radiant fraction): B3026 34.3 m with theta = 90, 49.7 m with theta = 16 (DNV 38.9 m: -12 % and +28 %); B3029 42.6 and 63.5 m (DNV 50.0 m: -15 % and +27 %); SA8051 40.8 and 45.0 m (DNV 33.2 m: +23 % and +36 %). DNV's results lie between the amplified and unamplified toolkit values for the horizontal propane tests, so the horizontal angle factor as written in eq (4) does not reproduce DNV's published validation outputs within 10 % either way. The vertical case is unaffected. Acceptance for the model family should therefore be set at DNV's own accuracy (flame length about +/-33 %, radiation about +/-40 %), not 10 %.
- **Witlox et al., Hazards XXI (2009), "Two-phase jet releases and droplet dispersion: scaled and large-scale experiments, droplet-size correlation development and model validation"** (grade A for the description, correlation constants not transcribed). The Phase III correlation is a three-regime curve of Sauter mean diameter against superheat: mechanical break-up below transition point A, a transition between A and B, and a fully flashed value of 80 micron above point B, with a 10 micron cut-off. The Yellow Book formula used by the tool gives 8 to 23 micron for the hot leaks, an order of magnitude below the fully flashed 80 micron. A hand check shows this does not change the hot-leak rain-out: an 80 micron propane droplet takes about 18 s to fall 2 m but evaporates in well under a second (rough estimate, 0.14 s), so rain-out stays zero.
- **API 521**: "521 e6 PA.pdf" is the publisher's product page and table of contents for the sixth edition (no technical content). "521_e9-WI_11-20250610.pdf" is an API committee ballot draft of the ninth edition, marked as not an API Standard and not to be reproduced, circulated or quoted outside API committee activities. It was not used as a source and nothing was taken from it: a draft is not a design basis, and the notice restricts its use. If the owner is entitled to use it, say so and which clauses are wanted; the published edition remains the source to cite.
- **Roberts, Gosse and Hawksworth (HSL/BG Technology), Hazards XV paper 9 (xv-paper-09), "Thermal radiation from fireballs on failure of LPG storage vessels"** (read as to subject only): 2 tonne propane vessel BLEVE tests. Relevant to a fireball/BLEVE scenario, which this study does not model.
- **FERC, "Evaluation of DEGADIS 2.1 using Advisory Bulletin ADB-10-07"** (not analysed this round): dense-gas dispersion validation cases (LNG); relevant to the dispersion gap, not to LPG flammable distances directly.
- **adam.zip**: the Air Force ADAM dispersion model as 1990s DOS executables with a readme. The executables were not run (unvetted binaries; DOS programs); only the readme text was read.

## Ninth batch: BLEVE and the vessel failure modes (2026-11-10)

The book prints the final flux as 20,361 W/m2; its own factors give 20,257 (a 0.5 % slip in the book). Not implemented: transmissivity from the graphs (Figures 6.2, 6.3), and chapter 7 blast and fragments (Baker et al. graphs and tables).
- **Purple Book (PGS 3 / CPR 18E)** (grade A): Table 3.3 pressure vessel frequencies (instantaneous 5e-7, 10 minutes 5e-7, 10 mm hole 1e-5 per year; add 5e-6 to the first two if impact or operating error cannot be excluded; leak under a pressurised tank igniting 1e-6 per year); Table 4.5 direct ignition of an instantaneous release (average/high reactive gas: 0.2, 0.5, 0.7 for under 1000 kg, 1000 to 10,000 kg, over 10,000 kg); P_BLEVE = 0.7 given direct ignition for stationary installations; failure pressure 1.21 x relief opening pressure for external fire; Table 4.8 cloud mass fraction from the adiabatic flash fraction; BLEVE blast is 0.03 to 0.1 bar at the 35 kW/m2 distance.
- **Roberts, Gosse and Hawksworth (HSL/BG Technology), Hazards XV, IChemE Symposium Series 147, "Thermal radiation from fireballs on failure of LPG storage vessels"** (grade A): four 2-tonne propane vessels failed in a jet fire after the PRV (17.24 barg) opened, at 16.5 to 24.4 barg, 217 to 286 s after the start, with vapour-space wall temperatures 704 to 870 C. Released mass 279 to 1708 kg; measured fireball diameter 41 to 85 m, duration 3 to 7 s, average SEP at maximum projected area 117 to 312 kW/m2 (242 to 294 at maximum power up/downwind), incident flux at 50 to 200 m from the 1708 kg release 36.7 to 4.4 kW/m2 upwind and 47.8 to 5.1 kW/m2 crosswind. Roberts/CCPS D = 5.8 M^(1/3), t = 0.45 M^(1/3), and Prugh D = 6.48 M^0.325, t = 0.825 M^0.26, are quoted in the paper (the paper prints 0.825; the Yellow Book prints 0.852).
- **Validation (workbook `Propane_Failure_Modes.xlsx`, sheet HSL_Validation; not included in this repository)**: with the Yellow Book formulas, fireball diameter is within -14 % to +27 % of the measured values, duration within -16 % to +23 %, average SEP -35 % to +96 % (SEP varies in time; the paper warns against the value at maximum area), and the incident flux for the 1708 kg release with H = 2 r and transmissivity (6.29) is within -14 % to +13 % of the upwind measurements at 50 to 200 m (4 of 6 within 10 %). The model is therefore usable for screening distances to about +/-15 %; 9 of 29 individual comparisons are within 10 %.
- **Vapour cloud explosion and flash fire (Purple Book 4.8 and appendix 4.C.10, grade A)**: after ignition of an unconfined cloud, a pure flash fire with probability 0.6 or a pure explosion with 0.4 (LPG-Integraal: 0.3 and 0.7); cloud mass = mass within the LFL contour; explosion by the multi-energy method at the highest blast strength (class 10) with scaled distances 1.5 (0.3 barg) and 3 (0.1 barg): R = 1.5 or 3 times (f_obstr E / Pa)^(1/3), f_obstr default 0.08, centred on the cloud; the book states this equals a TNT-equivalent method with a factor of 20 % between 10 and 100 kPa and reproduces the older 0.03 and 0.06 (0.1 E)^(1/3) formulas. Implemented in `process_safety/vce.py` with the older-formula equivalence as a test. For propane the toolkit TNT route (eta 0.2, Kinney-Graham, no ground reflection) gives 26 % longer 0.3 barg distances than the Purple Book route. Rupture without BLEVE puts 97 % of the inventory in the cloud (Table 4.8); a 100 m3 bullet then gives 0.3 barg at 176 m and 0.1 barg at 352 m; the 10-minute jet release of the same vessel (flammable mass about 790 kg by the entrainment estimate) gives 46 m and 92 m. The Yellow Book multi-energy curves (Figure 5.8) and the blast lethality figure (Purple Book Figure 5.5) are graphs and are not reproduced.
- **Key finding**: the PRV does not prevent a BLEVE when the fire heats the vapour-space wall; all four HSL vessels failed after the PRV had opened.

## Tenth batch: DNV equation 58, industry norms and further papers (2026-11-10)

- **DNV JFSH eq 58 resolves the flame-length mismatch.** DNV's liquid / two-phase model takes the effective source diameter as Ds = 2 r_j sqrt(rho_j / rho_vap[Tsat(P0)]) (eq 58), not the pure-vapour eq 3. Implemented as `jet_flame.chamberlain(rho_vap_kg_m3=...)`; with it the toolkit reproduces DNV's Validation Table 13 flame lengths for B3026, B3029 and SA8051 within 2 % (`tests/test_jetfire_dnv.py`). DNV's own results are 25 %, 63 % and 81 % above the measured lengths. The earlier statement that the horizontal angle factor was unverified is withdrawn: the angle factor of eq 4 was correct. For vertical INERIS fires eq 3 matches within 10 % and eq 58 is 22 % to 33 % short. For the hot leaks the DNV-consistent options (eq 58 and the Cook radiant fraction) shorten the flame by 24 % to 27 % but raise the 1 % death reach by 24 % to 26 %. `lg_release` takes `fire.dnv_two_phase_source_diameter` and `fire.fs_model`; defaults are unchanged.
